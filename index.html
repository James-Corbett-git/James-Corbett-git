About

Field notes

I'm a PhD candidate in Applied Mathematics & Statistics at Stony Brook University, advised by Roman Samulyak. My work centers on the numerical simulation of pellet fueling and disruption mitigation systems for tokamak fusion reactors — problems that sit at the intersection of magnetohydrodynamics, kinetic particle transport, and large-scale parallel computing.

Day to day, that means building and extending C++ simulation codes on adaptive, distributed-memory meshes: tracking how solid pellets ablate into cold, dense plasma clouds, and how that plasma redistributes itself across thousands of MPI ranks in real time. I also bring a computational background from outside plasma physics, having previously worked on a Python/ML internship at Zencastr.

Research Focus

Where the problems live

Pellet Fueling & Disruption Mitigation

Simulating how injected pellets ablate and redistribute mass and impurities in tokamak plasmas — core physics behind ITER's fueling and disruption-mitigation strategy.

Low-Rm Magnetohydrodynamics

Numerical methods for low magnetic Reynolds number MHD, modeling the coupled fluid and electromagnetic behavior of partially ionized ablation clouds.

HPC & Adaptive Mesh Refinement

Parallel PDE solvers on p4est-based adaptive meshes, built for distributed-memory clusters using MPI — the computational backbone that makes the physics tractable at scale.

Selected Work

Projects

PelletSolver

Simulation Codebase

A parallel simulation codebase built on p4est adaptive mesh refinement and MPI, developed as part of ITER-funded pellet fueling research.

  • Particle redistribution pipeline spanning quadtree traversal, packing, and sparse all-to-all communication across MPI ranks
  • Space-filling-curve based mesh partitioning using Morton/Z-curve ordering for load balance across processors
  • Mesh rebuilt from scratch every timestep, requiring an efficient search–pack–communicate cycle each cycle
  • Gradient reconstruction on unbalanced adaptive trees using neighbor-quadrant data
C++MPIp4estAMR

plasmoid1d

Simulation Codebase

A C++ codebase modeling 1D plasma dynamics, tracking charge-state evolution and radiative cooling for pellet ablation clouds.

  • Charge-state evolution for neon using OpenADAS atomic rate coefficients (PLT/PRB, radiative cooling functions)
  • Polymorphic equation-of-state hierarchy spanning Saha-neon, polytropic, and stiffened-gas models
  • Heun's method time integration, with comparative analysis of implicit schemes (Crank–Nicolson, implicit midpoint) for stiff, oscillatory plasma systems
